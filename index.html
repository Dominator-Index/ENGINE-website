01 / THE IDEA

Think across scales.

Can we keep the efficiency of low-dimensional sampling while still using curvature information from the full parameter space?

Zeroth-order optimization fine-tunes language models using only forward passes. Full-space sampling is informative but costly in variance. Low-rank sampling is efficient, but its preconditioned update can miss curvature effects from the full space.

ENGINE connects these two scales through Variational MultiScale theory from computational mechanics. An endogenous V-cycle alternates low-rank and full-space optimization, while a subgrid correction brings fine-scale curvature feedback into the coarse update.

02 / THE METHOD

Small updates.
A wider perspective.

The curvature operator evolves with the model.
ENGINE refreshes the connection between scales as training progresses.

COARSE PHASE

Spend queries in a smaller space.

Represent the update with low-rank factors B and A. Sample in r(m + n) coordinates instead of mn full-space coordinates, and maintain a compact curvature model for each branch.

ΔW = BA · dc = r(m + n)

The dimension-dependent variance bound decreases under comparable gradient and preconditioning scales.

03 / THE PRINCIPLE

Reduce variance.
Correct bias.

Two complementary mechanisms improve the update.

01

Low-dimensional sampling controls variance.

The coarse parameter dimension is dc = r(m + n), compared with df = mn. Under comparable gradient and preconditioning scales, this reduces the dimension-dependent variance bound.

MSEideal = Vc ≤ Vc + ‖τsgs‖²
02

Subgrid feedback corrects curvature mismatch.

In the local-linear limit, exact deterministic correction removes the structural bias relative to the restricted fine-space target, while preserving coarse sampling variance. The paper also analyzes approximate corrections and establishes a stationarity bound under stated assumptions.
